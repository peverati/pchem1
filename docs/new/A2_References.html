Appendix B — Further Reading and References

This book covers the essentials at a level suitable for a first course. When you want a second explanation, more detail, or the topics that we only touched on, these are good places to look. They are grouped by purpose, roughly from the most accessible to the most advanced. Resources marked free are available online at no cost.

B.1 A second explanation, at the same level

  • McQuarrie and Simon (1997) and Atkins et al. (2018): the classic chemistry texts for this course, with clear derivations and many worked problems. Most topics in this book have a matching chapter in either one.
  • LibreTexts (n.d.) (free): open textbooks and course notes covering the same material, written by many different instructors. Useful when you want the same topic explained in different words.
  • MIT OpenCourseWare (2008) (free): lecture notes, problem sets, and exams from MIT’s thermodynamics and kinetics course.
  • Peverati (n.d.) (free): the companion volume, on quantum mechanics, atoms, molecules, and spectroscopy.

B.2 More depth on thermodynamics (chapters 1–13)

  • Callen (1985): the classic text on the logical structure of thermodynamics, from its postulates to the thermodynamic potentials and stability.
  • Smith et al. (2005): the standard engineering text, with a thorough treatment of non-ideal gases, phase equilibria, and solutions.

B.3 Chemical kinetics (chapter 14)

  • Houston (2001): a readable introduction that goes from rate laws to collision theory, transition-state theory, and reaction dynamics.
  • Espenson (1995): a practical text on how rate laws are measured and how mechanisms are deduced from them.
  • Laidler (1987): the classic comprehensive text, covering both the experimental and the theoretical side of kinetics.
  • Steinfeld et al. (1999): a more advanced text on the theories of reaction rates and on reaction dynamics.

B.4 Mathematics

  • McQuarrie (2003): written by a chemist for chemists, covering the calculus used in this book and much more.

B.5 Data

  • National Institute of Standards and Technology (n.d.) (free): experimental thermochemical data for thousands of substances, including heat capacities, enthalpies of formation, and entropies.
